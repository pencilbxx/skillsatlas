## Short version for the team

Martina was clear on the jobseeker side: **the client chooses where they want to work.** She can guide and talk about transferable skills. She does not decide the job for them.

The **gap between appointments** is not something she described as a designed “three-week programme.” She said it comes down to **caseload / how much time she has.**

She does **not** see big holes in how her office works. She said the process works well there, and that it is a strong office. She did not give numbers.

On **AI replacing the person:** she said a lot of clients are on a long-term payment and do not want to work. She does not think AI can change that. That work is encouragement and talking through “will I be better off in a job?” She said **you definitely need a person.** Coaching and case management matter.

They already use **some** AI-ish tools in the gap (she named **Flex** and **TN Connect** for interview prep). She did not describe a 2026 tech roadmap.

On the **SkillsAtlas demo** she recognised the problem on the first screen (“you’ve been assessed, here’s a course list” — that could probably be better). On Evidence she followed the extra questions about the person’s experience and said that evidence work would be **really, really good if it beats keyword matching**. Two roles instead of a big list: **quite good, because it saves them time**. Last aha: the **voice AI helper** on the board (“pretty cool actually”).

She did not say “please put this in our office.” After the call: she will **pass it to her managers** to see if it could fit their workflow; **as an external tool it will not work**; she suggested **focusing on recruitment agencies**.

The second half of the call was mostly that agency problem: searching your **own CRM** is slow; job titles lie; you can lose two hours and still have no candidate; **time is money**. A cheaper, inside-the-system way to find **evidence in CVs** (not just keywords) is the pain she talked about there.

## What Martina said (in order)

### 1. The adviser does not choose the job

Andrew asked whether the adviser can decide a job for the applicant.

Martina: **no.** For her, the **client decides** where they want to work. She can **guide** and give ideas on **transferable skills**. Everyone is different. It depends on what the client would like.

### 2. What support looks like after assessment

Andrew asked how much she can advise on **local jobs that might suit**, and what kind of support they give.

What she described was support work, not a yes/no on “we pick local jobs in meeting 1”:

- interview support and confidence
- speaking to employers / a pitch to an employer
- **in-person mock interview** practice
- other training: jobs, toolkits, interviews, CV, personal development

She did not say how often someone is an obvious instant match to a local vacancy.

### 3. AI vs a person (jobseeker / long-term clients)

Andrew asked how she would feel if part of the job were AI.

Martina:

- It depends on the **cohort**.
- A lot of clients are on a **long-term payment** and **do not want to work**.
- She does **not** think AI can “manipulate that.”
- A lot of the work is **encouragement**, and talking through the benefits of full-time work vs “it will not work for me” / “I will not be financially better off.”
- She said you get a lot of that (the notes also have “scores” here; the meaning is unclear, so it is not turned into a metric).
- **You definitely need a person.**
- **Coaching** is a major part of managing the case.

### 4. The three-week wait

Andrew asked if there is a reason for the gap between appointments. He compared it to **C-Tech** (as heard) having about a 20-day window, and asked if it was a protocol.

Martina: **realistically it comes down to how much time her caseload allows.**

She did **not** say that three weeks is mandatory, or that Turas Nua designed three weeks because it is better for jobseekers. She did not give office data on the wait, or on how many people do not come back after assessment 1.

Andrew asked how she would feel about sending the person away for those weeks **with something that helps**, including AI.

Martina: they **already use that in some facets** — **Flex**, and AI to support a **tailored interview for specific industries**.

### 5. TN Connect (as she described it)

Andrew asked what the system was. She named **TN Connect**.

As she described it:

- if someone is going for an interview, she can **add interview videos**
- it would “cast a complete story”
- it can look up a **tailored version of the interview for specific industries**
- she **leaves it as a task**

That is all we have on what it includes. No comment in numbers on whether it gets people jobs.

### 6. Gaps in their process

Andrew asked if she thinks there are gaps in the process.

Martina: **no.** She does not. She thinks it **works well** for this party. She said they are a **very strong office**.

Andrew referred to something from earlier: better success metrics than other offices. She agreed it would be **very strong**, and said that comes down to **previous backgrounds** / what they have done before.

**No success rate, no comparison numbers, no “our data says X.”** Do not put a figure on this.

### 7. The SkillsAtlas demo

Andrew walked the live sitting with her (https://skillsatlas.vercel.app).

**Problem.** When she first saw the page, she clearly identified it: **you’ve been assessed, here’s a course list.** Her take: that **probably could be better.**

**Evidence.** She went to the next screen, acknowledged what was happening, and when she saw that **more questions can be asked about the person’s experience**, she clicked through and answered the demo. She said this evidence piece would be **really, really good if it beats keyword matching.**

**Two routes.** She saw **exactly two next roles**, not a job board. She said that is **quite good**, because it **saves time for them** instead of a lot of choices. She clicked **Choose this path**.

**Board.** That opened the STAR piece on the board. Last aha: the **AI helper** — more questions, answers from the CV, voice, help with the presentation. She said **“Oh, that’s pretty cool actually.”**

### 8. Recruitment agencies (where the call went)

The talk moved to agencies filling roles (Andrew named **Air Business Talent** as heard), and to **LinkedIn Recruiter**.

Martina said she **does not think** she has seen LinkedIn Recruiter.

Then, mixed with Andrew’s explanation, the problem she engaged with was:

- agencies compete for the **same jobs**
- each agency has its **own CRM** with a lot of clients
- searching that CRM is **hard**
- you can have “golden nuggets” sitting in it
- agencies are a **mixture** of big and small

**Problem in her words (agencies):** not using what they already have. Limited **emails per month**, so you have to be smart. You can send a hundred emails and get **zero replies**. Then you go back to your own CRM. It is **very hard to filter without opening each CV**. Someone can have the title **systems administrator** but only **training**, not the experience. You have to open files to see.

**What “success” sounded like there:** not just finding a person on LinkedIn — **filtering through the CV itself** (keywords / what is actually in the CV).

Andrew asked: what if it is not only keywords — if an agent is unsure, it **asks a few more questions** and you get better context?

She agreed that would be **even better**. Saving time landed as the **gold phrase**: you go down a rabbit hole for **two hours**, you have **no candidate**, and **time is money in recruitment**.

**Who to sell to / who not to start with**

- The conversation landed on: **bigger money is in recruitment agencies, not employability companies.** Martina agreed (“Yeah, got it”).
- Smaller agencies were described as interesting to reach out to.
- **AirEvo** (as heard): **would not start there.** They have their **own IT team**. Recruitment is only a **branch** of a big company.
- Something like this would have to sit **inside their systems** for **security and privacy** (said in the agency / “Sunseat tech” as-heard stretch).

**Price**

Andrew asked how much companies would pay.

Martina: **she has no idea.** That is something to look up. They already pay for things like **Salesforce**. **LinkedIn Recruiter** she remembered as **thousands**, **per month**, depending on **version**, how many **recruiters**, and how many **job advertisements**. You might only get **10 job ads** a month on it; more costs more. People also **swap ads** (take one down, put another up). Jobs go on **more than LinkedIn** — she listed LinkedIn, Irish Jobs, Indeed, computer jobs, and said she would probably put a job in about **eight places**. That is a cost on all of them.

She did not name a price for SkillsAtlas.

### 9. After Andrew left

As he walked away:

- she will **pass this information to her managers** to see if it could **fit in their workflow**
- **as an external tool it will not work**
- she suggested **focus on the agencies**

That is a goodbye, not a signed next step.
